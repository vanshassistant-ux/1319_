# Copyright

## In 30 Seconds

In a general U.S. copyright-law overview, copyright protects eligible original expression once it is fixed in a tangible medium, such as writing, a recording, or a photograph. It does not give ownership of an idea, fact, method, system, or concept simply because one appears in a protected work. Copyright includes specified exclusive rights, subject to limits and exceptions. A particular use requires fact-specific legal analysis.

## Why This Matters

Copyright concepts arise in presentations, websites, software, video, music, and marketing. Separating protected expression from an underlying subject or idea helps students ask better questions. This is general education, not an assessment of infringement, fair use, registration, licensing, or a real dispute.

## Learning Objectives

- Define copyright in a high-level U.S. legal context.
- Distinguish expression from ideas, facts, methods, and systems.
- Explain fixation and originality.
- Identify high-level exclusive rights and limits.
- Describe fair use as fact-specific.

## The College Version

### Expression, originality, and fixation

Copyright concerns eligible original works of authorship fixed in a tangible medium. A writing, recording, photograph, film, software file, or architectural drawing can illustrate fixation. Originality means independent human creation with at least a minimal creative contribution; it does not require novelty, artistic acclaim, or commercial success. This is a U.S.-qualified starting point, not a verdict on any particular work.

### Ideas are not owned by copyright

Copyright protects eligible expression, not ideas, facts, systems, procedures, methods, processes, concepts, principles, or discoveries as such. A person may write an original article about a historical event, but the event's facts remain available for others to explain in their own words. Likewise, an app idea is not the same as the app's original illustrations, text, photographs, or code. Names, titles, slogans, and short phrases are also not ordinarily copyright subject matter, although source-identifying marks can raise separate trademark questions.

### Rights and limits belong in the same picture

The U.S. framework identifies exclusive rights connected to reproduction, derivative works, distribution, and certain public performances and displays. These rights are subject to statutory limitations, agreements, licenses, and factual context. Registration is separate from the general rule that protection arises upon fixation; it can have important legal effects without being the moment copyright always begins. This lesson does not supply a registration or enforcement plan.

### Fair use needs a fact-specific analysis

Fair use can permit certain unlicensed uses under U.S. law, but it is not a percentage rule. Courts consider the use's purpose and character, the nature of the copyrighted work, the amount and substantiality used, and effects on a potential market. A small excerpt is not automatically fair, and educational or nonprofit context does not automatically settle the result. A student can identify the factors in a hypothetical without deciding whether a real use is fair or infringing.

### Related IP categories have different purposes

Copyright concerns eligible original expression. Trademark law concerns signs that identify and distinguish source. Patent law addresses qualifying inventions in a different legal system. A business can have a brand name, original product photographs, and a technical product feature at once, but sorting these assets does not determine that any right exists or what someone should do.

## Key Vocabulary

- **Copyright:** A U.S. legal framework that protects eligible original expression fixed in a tangible medium, subject to limits.
- **Originality:** Independent human creation with a minimal degree of creativity.
- **Fixation:** Capture in a stable medium that can be perceived, reproduced, or communicated beyond a transitory period.
- **Expression:** The particular authored words, images, sounds, or arrangement used to communicate content.
- **Idea-expression distinction:** The boundary between eligible expression and unowned ideas, facts, methods, or systems.
- **Fair use:** A fact-specific U.S. doctrine that can permit some unlicensed uses after statutory-factor analysis.

## Eli-10

Copyright protects the particular way someone makes and records a creative work. If you draw an original dog in a raincoat and save the drawing, the lines and colors are your expression. The broad idea of a dog in a raincoat is still available for someone else to draw in a different way. Fair use is not a coupon that makes any short use safe; courts look at the actual situation.

## Eli's Analogy

Think of a recipe card. Its original writing, photographs, and layout are like expression. The broad idea of tomato soup is not owned by the person who made one card.

The comparison has limits: real copyright questions can involve ownership, agreements, statutory limitations, and facts the recipe card does not show.

## Worked Example

A fictional café posts an original food photograph and an article about seasonal ingredients. A competing café may discuss the same ingredients and write a new explanation, because facts and general topics are not automatically owned by the first café. Copying the photograph or distinctive wording is a different question from independently creating new expression. For a repost hypothetical, a student can name fair-use factors but should not declare a real use fair or infringing.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mistake:** Copyright protects an idea once it is written down. **Correction:** It may protect eligible original expression, not the underlying idea.
- **Mistake:** Registration always creates copyright. **Correction:** In the general U.S. account, eligible original expression is protected upon fixation; registration is a separate issue.
- **Mistake:** A small amount is automatically fair use. **Correction:** Fair use is fact-specific and has no preset safe percentage.
- **Mistake:** A brand name, an invention, and a photograph are the same IP category. **Correction:** Trademark, patent, and copyright address different subjects.

## Compare / Contrast

- **Expression vs. idea:** eligible original expression may be protected; the underlying idea is not owned by copyright.
- **Copyright vs. trademark:** copyright concerns expression; trademark concerns source identification.
- **Fair-use framework vs. percentage rule:** statutory factors and circumstances matter; no fixed amount resolves every case.

## Key Takeaway

In a general U.S. overview, copyright protects eligible original expression once it is fixed, not underlying ideas or facts. Analyze rights and limits with applicable law and real facts rather than shortcuts.

## Practice Question Bank

1. What does fixation mean? **Answer:** A work is captured in a sufficiently stable medium.
2. Which is expression? **Answer:** An original app illustration, rather than the general app idea.
3. Does fewer than ten seconds of a song automatically establish fair use? **Answer:** No; fair use is fact-specific.
4. What does the exclusive-rights overview include? **Answer:** Specified rights such as reproduction and distribution, subject to limits.
5. How should a business sort a service name and original product photographs? **Answer:** Trademark concepts for the source-identifying name and copyright concepts for the photographs.
