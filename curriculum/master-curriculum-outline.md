ELIEXPLAINS.ORG
MASTER SUBJECT & TOPIC CURRICULUM OUTLINE
Use this outline as the content blueprint for EliExplains.org.
The goal is not to reproduce entire textbooks. Each subject should identify the concepts students are most likely to encounter in introductory college courses and break those concepts into focused, searchable lesson pages.

UNIVERSAL ELIEXPLAINS LESSON FORMAT
Treat each named topic below as one potential webpage.
Every completed lesson should ideally contain:
1. In 30 Seconds
A 40–100 word explanation of the essential idea.
Answer:
What should a student understand if they only have 30 seconds?

2. Learning Goals
Usually 2–5.
Example:
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
* define the major concept,
* recognize important terminology,
* explain how the pieces relate,
* apply the concept to a simple example.

3. The College Version
Approximately:
* simple topic: 500–800 words,
* typical topic: 700–1,100 words,
* complex topic: 1,000–1,500+ words.
Use correct college terminology.
Divide long explanations into subsections.

4. Key Vocabulary
Usually:
* 4–8 terms.
Each term receives a concise definition.

5. Eli-10 Explanation
Approximately 150–350 words.
Explain the same concept in plain language without making it childish.

6. Eli's Analogy
One memorable analogy or everyday comparison where appropriate.

7. Worked Example / Real Example
Use an example appropriate for the subject.
Examples:
* calculation,
* scenario,
* historical document,
* case,
* code snippet,
* graph,
* design problem,
* research example.

8. Common Mistakes
Usually 3–5 misconceptions.
For each:
Mistake: what students commonly misunderstand.
Correction: what is actually true.

9. Key Takeaway
30–80 words.
Answer:
What is the single most important thing to remember?

10. Quick Check
Initially:
* 3 multiple-choice questions.
Later:
* 5–10 on high-traffic lessons.
Every question should include an answer explanation.

11. Sources & Lesson Status
Include:
* openly licensed sources,
* authoritative references,
* applicable license information,
* attribution,
* last reviewed date,
* editorial/review status.

CONTENT DEPTH STANDARD
Do not aim for the same number of lessons in every subject.
Recommended:
Small subject
12–18 topics
Suitable for:
* narrow foundations,
* practical survey subjects.
Standard subject
18–30 topics
This should be the default.
Large foundational subject
30–50 topics
Suitable for:
* Psychology,
* History,
* Economics,
* Computer Science,
* Web Development,
* Nursing Foundations.
Avoid publishing 100 weak pages where 25 excellent pages would provide a better learning experience.

INITIAL RELEASE STRATEGY
For every new subject:
Stage 1
Publish:
10–15 foundational lessons.
Stage 2
Expand to:
20–30 lessons.
Stage 3
Add:
* intermediate concepts,
* advanced topics,
* additional examples,
* larger question banks,
* cross-topic study paths.
This allows EliExplains to grow into a large college-learning library without sacrificing quality.

CORE ELIEXPLAINS PRINCIPLE
Every lesson should answer two different student needs:
The student asking:
"What does my professor expect me to know?"
gets:
The College Version
And the student asking:
"Okay... but what does that actually mean?"
gets:
Eli-10
The site should always preserve that distinction.
